What psyllium is, and why purity is the whole conversation

Psyllium is the seed husk of Plantago ovata, grown across north Gujarat and western Rajasthan and processed around Unjha, which handles the majority of world supply. The husk is almost entirely soluble dietary fibre, which is why it ends up in laxatives, fibre supplements, gluten-free bakery and, at the industrial end, in animal feed and road construction.

The number that separates suppliers is purity — the mucilloid content, meaning how much husk and how little seed and fine dust is actually in the bag. It is graded at 85%, 95%, 98% and 99%. Purity drives swell volume, which is what your formulation actually cares about: 85% husk swells to 35 ml/g, 99% to 70 ml/g. We quote all four, and the certificate of analysis states which you are getting, per lot.

From seed to husk

Purity is won in the milling, so this is the part of the chain we watch hardest.

  1. Procurement

    Seed bought from the Unjha and Rajasthan mandis, screened on arrival for admixture and moisture.

  2. Cleaning

    De-stoning, air classification and magnetic separation to remove dust, stone and metal.

  3. Milling and separation

    Husk is separated from the seed core through successive grinding and sieving passes.

  4. Grading to purity

    Repeat passes lift the husk from 95% through 98% to 99%, with the residue becoming Kha-Kha powder.

  5. Testing

    Purity, swell volume, moisture, ash, microbiology and heavy metals tested per lot.

  6. Packing

    Compressed into food-grade liners and bagged, batch-coded against the retention sample.
